I am a **joint Ph.D. candidate in Agricultural Economics and Management** at **Wageningen University & Research (WUR)** and the **Graduate School of the Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences (GS-CAAS)**. My research focuses on how climate extremes affect agricultural production, farm decision-making, and food markets.

## Research Interests

My interests lie at the intersection of **agricultural economics, environmental economics, climate adaptation, and applied econometrics**. I study how temperature shocks influence farm performance and input use; how farmers adapt through changes in production and management; and how agricultural supply shocks transmit into commodity prices and food inflation.

I am particularly interested in combining **farm-level panel data, climate and geospatial information, and causal inference methods** to produce policy-relevant evidence for European and Chinese agricultural systems.

## Doctoral Research

**From Heat Shocks to Food Inflation: Farm Adaptation, Input Use, and Price Transmission in European and Chinese Crop Systems**

My doctoral project investigates three connected questions:

1. How do extreme heat shocks affect farm output, input use, productivity, and economic performance?
2. Which farm-level adaptation strategies help mitigate these effects, and what trade-offs do they involve?
3. How do production shocks propagate through agricultural markets into prices and food inflation?

The project is designed to examine European and Chinese crop systems, with an emphasis on farm-level evidence and the link between microeconomic adjustment and market outcomes.

**Supervision:** Dr. Yann de Mey (WUR) and Prof. Xiangdong Hu (CAAS).

## Methods and Research Approach

My empirical interests include panel data models, difference-in-differences and event-study designs, instrumental variables, and approaches that integrate agricultural microdata with climate and spatial datasets.

I also have interests in livestock production, nutrient management, water quality, and the environmental effects of agricultural policy.
